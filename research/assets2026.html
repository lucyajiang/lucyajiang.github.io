Works Presented

I will be presenting our demo of ArtScape during Poster Session A (Monday, October 26). Please stop by!

Public art fundamentally shapes urban environments, fostering community identity and providing cultural and aesthetic enrichment. However, most urban artwork is inaccessible to blind and low vision (BLV) people. We introduce ArtScape, an urban art exploration prototype that allows users to discover, inspect, and explore urban artwork throughout a city, powered by a novel VLM-based pipeline that automatically detects and characterizes artwork from street-view imagery. We present the design of ArtScape and findings from a user study evaluation including three BLV participants.

I was also a supporting author on Yotam's poster, See What I Mean: A Technology Probe of AI Assistants for Chart Exploration with Low Vision!

Charts remain inaccessible to low vision individuals (LVI), who commonly magnify local details at the cost of the overview. AI assistants can help, but prior work has largely served non-visual access, while LVI prefer using residual vision. We built ChartAEye, a technology probe that lets LVI interact with an AI assistant visually, sharing their magnified view and point of interest as system context. Using the probe, we then asked whether the AI's feedback also needs to be adapted to low vision, comparing a generic AI persona (GAI) with one designed around LVI needs and lived experience (LVAI). In a within-subjects study, 12 LVI used ChartAEye to complement their vision, reduce the effort of viewing chart elements, and practice more independence. Eight of 12 participants preferred the LVAI, whose responses more often carried spatial guidance (71.2% vs. 7.8%) and semantic guidance of visuals (84.8% vs. 29.1%). We close with design recommendations for AI assistants that support LVI in exploring charts with their residual vision.
